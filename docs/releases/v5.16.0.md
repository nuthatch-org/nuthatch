Nuthatch 5.16.0 adds writer failover (RFC-0078): a standby that takes over a dataset when its writer
dies, without an operator. It is off unless a mount's publish configuration asks for it, and a runtime
that does not is the runtime 5.15.0 was. There is no storage migration, and no NID, registry hash or
sealed segment changes. Upgrade followers before writers (below): the feed carries two new ops.

## Writer failover

Every host runs `nuthatch dev` over the same `mounts.toml`, publishing to one mirror:

```toml
[runtime.publish]
target = "s3://bucket/prefix"
followers = true

[runtime.publish.failover]
term = "10s"
standby = true
follow = "http://writer.internal:8288"   # optional
registry = "s3://bucket/registry"        # with follow
```

The writer holds a lease at `<data_identity>/lease.json` in the mirror and renews it a third of a
term at a time. Every change to it is a conditional put on the bytes last seen, so two hosts racing
for one lease produce one holder. A standby takes it once it has gone unrenewed for the term and half
again, measured on the standby's own clock: no two hosts' clocks are ever compared.

- **A writer that loses its lease stops.** Its store is fenced off, so its next commit fails; the
  nest is quarantined with the reason on `/<route>/ready`; its publisher stops; and the catalogue is
  not moved while the mirror's lease names another fence. A follower that has followed the new writer
  refuses the old one, by the fence on each feed `mount` line.
- **Taking over, cold.** A standby with no copy seeds an empty dataset from the mirror's seed snapshot
  and replays the unsealed gap from the chain.
- **Taking over, warm.** A standby with `follow` follows the writer while it waits and, on taking the
  lease, installs that copy as its store: rows, catalogue and verified segments from one generation.
  It resumes from the next block with nothing to replay.
- **A writer that took over from behind** holds its catalogue until every table is sealed through the
  mirror's, so the mirror's history never goes backwards.
- **Webhooks** survive a takeover: the outbox travels on the feed, so a promoted standby delivers what
  the old writer had not. Delivery across a takeover is at least once.
- **A planned move** is `POST /_admin/failover/handover`: the writer stops committing, waits for the
  mirror to hold what it sealed, and releases the lease, which a standby takes at once.
- **Metrics**, per nest with failover on: `nuthatch_lease_fence`, `nuthatch_lease_held`,
  `nuthatch_lease_renewals_total`, `nuthatch_lease_renew_failures_total`.
- `serve --follow --mirror ... --standby` reports each dataset's lease on `/ready` from a follower,
  and takes nothing.

`docs/operators.md` has the whole of it under "Failover".

**Measured** by `scripts/failover-gate.sh`, new and run in CI as `failover`: two to five `dev`
processes over a fixture chain and one mirror, at a 3-second term, on a MacBook. A second writer is
refused while the first renews. A writer frozen with `SIGSTOP` is taken over, and on waking stops by
itself. From `SIGKILL` of the writer to the standby's first full answer: 5 to 6 s cold, 5 s warm.
A planned handover: 1 to 2 s from the request. At the default 10-second term a crash costs about
16 s. `a_promoted_follower_ends_where_an_uninterrupted_writer_does` cuts a writer while every row is
hot, after a reorg and after a seal, and in each the promoted writer ends with the rows of one never
interrupted.

### Limits

- **The mirror must refuse a stale conditional write**, or there is no lease. versitygw, which CI
  runs, does; Garage and Zenko CloudServer accept one. Run
  `tests/e2e_minio_publish.rs::the_store_enforces_conditional_writes` against your store first.
- **A waiting standby serves nothing** until it takes the lease, so a load balancer sees it as down.
  Serve reads from followers.
- **Failover is taken at boot.** A failover mirror set on a live mount, or by
  `PATCH /_admin/mounts`, is refused with "restart the runtime".
- The parity run covers an ERC-20 nest at three chosen points; a factory nest's child registry is
  rebuilt from stored events on promotion, which is reasoned and not yet run.

## For a runtime without failover

Nothing to do, and these are the only differences:

- A store whose ownership another holder has claimed (`LostOwnership`) is now a terminal fault for
  the nest on it, quarantined until restart, where it was retried on a backoff that failed the same
  way each time. This reaches scaled mode's workers too.
- A conditional put the mirror refuses is reported as its own error type; the messages are as they
  were.
- The feed carries alert-outbox enqueues and deliveries as ops, and snapshots carry the outbox, so
  a follower holds it beside the rows and is charged for it.

Three RFCs are accepted and not built: RFC-0079 (Arrow results: `/sql` in Arrow IPC, Flight SQL
behind a feature), RFC-0080 (partial sync for the embeddable engine), and RFC-0081 (the engine in
use).

## Compatibility and upgrade

Replace the binary and restart. A 5.15 store is used as it is, and no NID, registry hash or sealed
segment changes.

**Upgrade followers before writers.** A 5.16 writer sends two ops, `outbox` and `unoutbox`, that a
5.15 follower cannot read, and it sends them whenever an alert is enqueued or delivered. A 5.16
follower reads a 5.15 writer. This is the order the rolling-upgrade procedure in
`docs/operators.md` already takes, and `scripts/rolling-upgrade.sh` gates it.
