Nuthatch 5.20.0 moves the SQL engine to Burrmill 0.0.1, the first tagged revision, and DataFusion
from 55.1 to 55.2 with it. It adds no nuthatch feature. There is no storage migration, and no NID,
registry hash or sealed segment moves. A few `/sql` answers and refusals read differently, which is
why this is a minor and not a patch.

## Upgrade guidance

Replace the binary and restart. A 5.19 store is used as it is.

- **Anything that matches on a refusal's text**: a statement refused for memory now begins
  `limit exceeded:` where it began `substrate error:`. A statement stopped at its spill cap reads as
  it did.
- **A view or query that casts to `TIMESTAMP`**: read the section below. A fraction finer than a
  microsecond is cut off.
- **A view that calls `list_sum` over integers or decimals**: the answer is exact and no longer a
  `DOUBLE`.
- Every other nest: nothing to do.

## What a `/sql` caller can see

- **A limit is named as a limit.** A statement over its memory budget answers `limit exceeded:`
  where it answered `substrate error:`, which is what a damaged segment also says. A statement
  stopped at its spill cap keeps nuthatch's own refusal, unchanged.
- **A cast to `TIMESTAMP` is microseconds**, as DuckDB's is. `CAST(x AS TIMESTAMP)`, `TRY_CAST` and
  `::TIMESTAMP` without a precision, and `DATETIME`, are `TIMESTAMP(6)`. It was nanoseconds, so the
  same instant printed with up to nine fractional digits: one nanosecond before the epoch was
  `1969-12-31T23:59:59.999999999Z` and is now `1969-12-31T23:59:59.999999Z`. `block_timestamp`
  columns are integers and are not affected.
- **`list_sum` over integers or decimals is exact.** It summed every list as a `DOUBLE`, so
  `list_sum([1, 2, 3])` was `6.0` and a large sum lost digits. It now sums to `DECIMAL(38, s)` and
  refuses past 38 digits. `list_min` exists, and `list_min` and `list_max` answer NULL for a NULL
  list where they refused.
- **A statement reads at most 128 relations**, down from 256. A chain of 256 joins planned for
  14 s with no point at which a cancel could reach it; 128 plans in 1.6 s. The widest view in the
  six production nests reads 65.
- **A scan is charged for the segments it opens, as it opens them.** A scan reading a text column
  was charged nothing for its segments, and every other scan was charged for all of them before its
  first row. Under a tight `max_rss_mb` or tenant share a statement can therefore be refused where
  5.19 let it through, and a statement reading one table several times holds less at the start.

Strict isolation's function list follows: `list_min` is admitted and `address_bytes`, which Burrmill
no longer registers, is gone from it.

Burrmill's [0.0.1 changelog](https://github.com/nuthatch-org/burrmill/blob/v0.0.1/CHANGELOG.md) has
the rest, most of which a nest never sees.

## How it was checked

The release gate ran on the six production nest copies against 5.17.2: every statement answered, and
no answer differed. QoS answers all nine of its statements at 704 MB without a spill directory. The
allocations dashboard peaks at 1010 MiB against 937 before, inside the 2 GB cursor budget.
